# Why Managing Security Concerns Matters

This document explains the context for the [Managing Security Concerns Policy](managing-security-concerns.md). The policy remains the authoritative source for requirements.

## Scope and purpose

A security concern is an issue related to the security of an application. It may be identified by a team during development, formal penetration testing, security analysis tooling, or a security bulletin. The source policy names static and dynamic analysis, software composition analysis, infrastructure-as-code and container scanning, third-party dependencies, and penetration testing as examples of discovery sources.

The policy provides a common process for managing security concerns related to UKHO software and helping to ensure application security. Concerns are assessed, prioritised, addressed and continuously monitored.

## Understanding the risk measures

The assessment uses three measures to describe risk at different points:

- **Inherent Risk** is the baseline risk before preventative measures are taken.
- **Residual Risk** is the remaining risk after mitigations are in place.
- **Target Risk** is the risk appetite the team, application or product is willing to accept.

Considering all three provides a standard score for a concern and distinguishes its baseline, mitigated and intended risk. The [how-to guide](managing-security-concerns-how-to.md) describes when and how the assessment is carried out.
