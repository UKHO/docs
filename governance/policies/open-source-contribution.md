# External Open Source Contribution Policy

This policy applies to UKHO contributions to external, third-party open source projects. It also applies to personal contributions that relate directly to an engineer's UKHO work or to UKHO's business, including marine geospatial systems and information for the mariner, even when made on personal equipment or in personal time. Other personal open source contributions are permitted without reference to this policy.

## Requirements

### Project and investment

Before contributing, review the project's documentation, contribution guidelines, and existing issues. Ensure the project aligns with UKHO business needs and that the contribution will not compromise UKHO intellectual property.

Investment in open source will normally be part of a wider project's investment appraisal or business case, in line with the delivery framework's Secure Funding goal. If it is not part of a wider project, it is a separate change and requires a separate business case.

### Licensing and approval

- The project must be governed by an OSI-approved licence. Verify that the licence permits the intended use and contribution.
- Obtain authorization from the Head of Engineering before contributing to any open source repository. Significant changes require re-authorization.
- Obtain the Head of Engineering's approval for contributions that may involve valuable intellectual property, such as a Contributor Licence Agreement (CLA).

### Quality and contribution practices

Contributions must meet the same quality standards as internal projects. This includes adhering to coding standards and best practices, writing unit tests, and providing clear documentation for changes.

Forking a project and making changes without contributing back is discouraged. Aim to contribute enhancements back to the original project.

The default is to provide minimal support for open source contributions. Do not invite bug reports or pull requests unless explicitly authorized. If support is necessary, ensure a governance process is in place.

Do not disclose sensitive or proprietary information in contributions. Follow security guidelines to ensure no vulnerabilities are introduced into the codebase.

Maintain documentation of each contribution, including its purpose, approvals obtained, and the licensing terms under which it is made.

### Procurement

Paying a supplier to make enhancements to an open source codebase is a procurement exercise. It must be carried out through the UKHO Procurement team, who will ensure that the business is competed where appropriate and that full long-term costs are considered.
