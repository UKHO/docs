# Why Package Adoption Matters

This document explains the rationale in the [Package Adoption
Policy](package-adoption.md). The policy remains the authoritative
source for requirements.

## Why this matters

UKHO has a [code reuse policy](code-reuse.md) that encourages reuse
of assets where possible. Packages can support this aim, but they
need ongoing development attention and maintenance.

When a team changes or moves on, a package can become orphaned. In
that state, it may receive attention only if an integrating team
identifies a vulnerability or if the package can no longer be used
because it depends on an out-of-support language version. This
creates risk for the teams that rely on the package and for the
business.

## What adoption is meant to achieve

The adoption process is intended to make the maintenance need
visible before a package is accepted. It quantifies the work
involved in bringing a package to an acceptable state for initial
adoption and in providing the ongoing capacity required to maintain
it.

This helps the business understand the level of work involved and
match that work to an adopting team. It also recognises that
packages may already be orphaned or may become orphaned in the
future because of business change.

## Governance of adoption

The [adoption checklist](../../software-engineering-policies/
PackageAdoption/PackageAdoptionChecklist.md) records the analysis
outcomes, and the Support Committee reviews the proposed adoption.
A team then accepts responsibility for the package and creates
stories based on that assessment.
